# How to fix your entire life in 1 day

## Summary

Dan Koe's essay connects identity, goals, and behavioral feedback to a one-day
reflection exercise. It proposes examining unwanted patterns, defining a desired
direction, and translating that direction into projects and daily actions.

## Why this was saved

- A structured reference for periodic personal reviews that connect long-term
  intentions with observable behavior and concrete next steps.

## Notes

### Source claims

- Koe argues that lasting change involves identity and underlying motives,
  rather than resolutions or discipline alone. He draws on developmental models
  and a feedback-loop view of learning and action.
- The exercise moves from morning reflection on dissatisfaction and possible
  futures, through daytime interruptions of habitual behavior, to evening
  synthesis and planning.
- The resulting plan combines an unwanted future, a desired future, a yearly
  goal, a monthly project, daily priorities, and limits on acceptable sacrifices.
- The closing game analogy frames projects as challenges and daily actions as
  quests. The author explicitly does not guarantee the exercise works for everyone.

### Editor synthesis

Useful as a journaling and planning framework, not evidence that a life can be
fixed in a day. Broad claims about identity, intelligence, and psychological
development are the author's interpretation, not established findings verified
by this note. Allow for real external constraints rather than treating every
obstacle as a personal failure.
