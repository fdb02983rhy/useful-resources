# Database Design Course

## Summary

An eight-hour introductory course by Caleb Curry on planning relational databases.
It develops a working vocabulary for databases and SQL, then spends most of its
time on relationships, keys, entity-relationship modeling, normalization, indexes,
data types, and joins.

## Why this was saved

- Provides one continuous path from basic database concepts to schema design.
- Gives extended treatment to relationship modeling and the tradeoffs among key types.
- Uses normalization and joins to connect logical design with query behavior.

## Notes

### Source claims

- Database design organizes stored data and its relationships according to a data model.
- The course distinguishes one-to-one, one-to-many, and many-to-many relationships
  and demonstrates how to represent each with tables and keys.
- Its key section covers primary, alternate, natural, surrogate, foreign, simple,
  composite, and compound keys, including constraints and selection tradeoffs.
- Later sections introduce entity-relationship cardinality and modality, the first
  three normal forms, clustered and nonclustered indexes, and several join forms.

### Editor synthesis

The unusually long runtime is useful for learners who want repetition and worked
examples. Experienced developers may prefer to jump directly to the relationship,
key, normalization, or join chapters.
