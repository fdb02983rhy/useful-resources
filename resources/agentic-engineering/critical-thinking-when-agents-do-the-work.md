# Critical thinking when agents do the work

## Summary

Addy Osmani argues that delegating execution to agents should not mean giving up
judgment or accountability. The essay proposes deliberate habits for framing
tasks, verifying evidence, reviewing decisions, and preserving essential skills.

## Why this was saved

- A practical review checklist for maintaining ownership when agents produce
  work faster than a person can inspect it.

## Notes

### Source claims

- Osmani distinguishes useful cognitive offloading from deferring to outputs
  and eventually accepting them without understanding or checking them.
- Form a hypothesis before asking, seek counterarguments, and define completion
  evidence upfront. Examine what a passing check proves and what it misses.
- Review assumptions and decisions, not just polished summaries or diffs;
  reconstruct relevant understanding and sample original sources when needed.
- Write clear briefs with constraints and acceptance criteria, limit work to
  review capacity, and deliberately practice skills worth retaining. Execution
  and auditing can be delegated, but accountability remains with people.

### Editor synthesis

Useful as a risk-based workflow checklist rather than a demand to inspect every
line manually. Independent evidence matters because implementation and tests can
share the same mistaken assumption. The essay's cited research was not separately
evaluated for this note.
